## (a) Executive summary

- The corpus's central physical fact matches our skill's premise: modern kernels are overwhelmingly **memory-bound** because compute has outgrown bandwidth [FlashAttention], [Roofline/Nsight Compute].
- FlashAttention wins by *never materializing* O(N²) intermediates in HBM — fusing matmul+softmax+mask+dropout into one tiled kernel driven by SRAM-capacity block equations [FlashAttention]. This is the canonical grounding for our Tier-3 "compute-in-graph vs materialize" question.
- FlashAttention-3 turns the same silicon from 35% to ~75% utilization purely through software structure: warp specialization, producer/consumer async pipelining, TMA (`setmaxnreg`, mbarriers) [FA-3], [H100 whitepaper] — composition is where user-controllable headroom lives (our T3 bet).
- Roofline reasoning (intensity, ridge point, ceilings) decides *which* optimizations can matter at all; hierarchical refinements are required for honest attribution on modern parts [Roofline/Nsight Compute], [Hopper µbench].
- Occupancy-maximization is explicitly debunked: ILP hides latency with fewer warps, forced occupancy causes register spills; Hopper decouples latency hiding from occupancy entirely via TMA [CUDA Best Practices], [H100 whitepaper] — proxy metrics mislead, paired wall-clock decides.
- Mixed precision has a concrete safety recipe: FP32 master weights + FP32 accumulation + FP32 reductions in sensitive ops for FP16 [Micikevicius]; E4M3 for weights/activations vs E5M2 for range-critical tensors in FP8 [FP8 formats].
- Data-movement endpoints: GPUDirect Storage eliminates CPU bounce buffers with a graceful compatibility fallback [GPUDirect Storage]; PagedAttention's block-paged KV cache trades a 20–26% slower kernel for 2–4× end-to-end throughput by eliminating fragmentation and enabling bigger batches [PagedAttention/vLLM].
- JAX-specific gold: `donate_argnums` sharp edges (invalidation, keyword args never donated, PyTree-wide donation) [JAX 0.11 ref]; name-based remat policies including residual offload to pinned host memory; XLA auto-remat is weak across `lax.scan` so scan bodies need manual checkpointing [JAX 0.11 ref] — directly applicable to xtrax's scan-heavy pipelines.
- Biology-workload patterns: ADEPT runs Smith-Waterman as anti-diagonal waves with warp shuffles, register spill points at warp boundaries, and traceback-free reverse scoring that avoids uncoalesced writes [ADEPT]; AlphaFold documents its own memory-reduction levers [AlphaFold SI]; MMseqs2 stays CPU/vectorized by design [MMseqs2].
- Most imitable methodology: locked clocks + many-run averages (FA-3: 1830 MHz, n=100) [FA-3]; median±spread reporting (Ansor n=5, FA n=10 mean±std) [Ansor], [FlashAttention]; roofline-ceiling overlays (MARLIN/Triton) [MARLIN], [Triton]; strongest-baseline pairing (Adobe production code, four specialized kernels) [Halide], [MARLIN]; mechanism-isolating ablation matrices [FA-3]; regime-shift sweeps [ADEPT].
- Sharpest cross-source tensions: fusion enthusiasm vs Halide-style separation of concerns [Halide], [Triton]; autotuning vs hand-tuning (10^720 schedule spaces vs Hopper-specific hand exploits) [Halide], [Ansor], [FA-3]; and the opaque-kernel cliff — Pallas/custom ops forfeit XLA fusion and memory planning [JAX 0.11 ref].

- Our T1 (host-boundary mechanics) has **no direct device-literature analogue** in this corpus; nearest analogies are FA-3's producer/consumer decoupling and GDS bounce-buffer elimination.
- Highest-value next artifacts for xtrax: (1) scan-body remat/checkpoint recipe in tier3 reference; (2) ProbeRecord run-variance + clock-lock fields before GPU re-runs; (3) explicit "baseline = strongest shipped config" rule in measurement protocol.

## (b) Memory hierarchy & hardware constraint facts

The corpus is remarkably consistent about the physical substrate every kernel technique responds to:

1. **Compute has outgrown bandwidth; most kernels are memory-bound.** GPU FLOPs have scaled much faster than memory bandwidth over the last decade, so deep-learning ops are increasingly bottlenecked by data movement rather than arithmetic [FlashAttention]. This is exactly the regime our Stage-0/Stage-1 probes operate in.
2. **Two-level cost model: SRAM fast, HBM slow, and the N×N intermediate is the killer.** Standard attention materializes S=QKᵀ and P=softmax(S) to HBM at O(N²); reading/writing those intermediates dominates wall-clock [FlashAttention]. The general lesson: *any* intermediate that round-trips through DRAM is a first-order cost, which is precisely what our Tier-3 "on-the-fly encode vs materialized array" question probes.
3. **Operational intensity and the ridge point decide which optimizations can matter.** Roofline: intensity = FLOPs/byte of DRAM↔SRAM traffic; left of the ridge point performance is bandwidth-throttled (fusion/reuse helps), right of it only compute-side tricks help [Roofline]. Intermediate "ceilings" (ILP limits, uncoalesced access, register pressure) must be broken one at a time to climb toward the roof [Roofline].
4. **Classical roofline is too flat for modern GPUs.** Real Hopper/Ampere parts have partitioned L2 with NUMA-like near/far hit/miss tiers, banked shared memory, and distributed shared memory (DSMEM), so hierarchical rooflines (DRAM vs L2 vs L1) are needed for honest attribution [Roofline]. Implication for us: a single "bytes moved" number is a simplification; where an access lands matters as much as how many bytes move.
5. **On-chip SRAM capacity dictates tiling.** FlashAttention derives block sizes directly from SRAM size M and head dim d (B_c = ⌈M/4d⌉, B_r = min(⌈M/4d⌉, d)) [FlashAttention]. Tile shapes are not aesthetic choices; they are capacity equations.
6. **Hopper-era asynchrony changes the ceiling, not the model.** FA-2 reached only ~35% utilization on H100; FA-3's warp specialization + async pipelining + TMA reach 740 TFLOPs/s FP16 (~75% util) and up to 1.2 PFLOPs/s FP8 [FA-3]. The gap between naive and hardware-aware execution on identical silicon is 2×+, entirely a software-structure effect — strong support for our premise that composition (T3), not hardware, is where user-controllable headroom lives.
7. **Register pressure is a real, manageable resource.** FA-3 uses Hopper's `setmaxnreg` to shift registers from producer warps (few needed) to consumer warps, preventing spills to high-latency local memory [FA-3].

## (c) Kernel technique inventory mapped to OUR tiers

### Tier 1 — host-boundary mechanics (taps, sinks, callbacks)

Direct literature coverage of *host-callback* mechanics is thin in this corpus — the sources are device-centric. What maps:

- **Producer/consumer role separation is the device-side analogue of our host-boundary concern**: FA-3 splits warps into dedicated producers (memory issue) and consumers (compute) precisely so neither stalls the other, and adds a dedicated dQ-writer so gradient accumulation doesn't contend with compute warps [FA-3]. Our ordered-tap-inside-scan problem (N serialized host round trips) is the same decoupling problem at the host boundary; the corpus says the fix pattern is "separate the mover from the computer," which is exactly what `async_indexed_stream` + staging sinks do.
- **Serialization tax on ordered operations**: both GPUDirect Storage and PagedAttention exist because a staging path serializes work that could overlap — GDS removes CPU bounce-buffer round trips between storage and GPU [GPUDirect Storage], and vLLM pages the KV cache so per-step work stops paying fragmentation taxes [PagedAttention/vLLM]. Same shape as our tap/sink problem: move bytes off the critical path or batch them.

### Tier 2 — data movement & prefetch

- **Fusion as IO elimination**: FlashAttention's entire win is refusing to round-trip intermediates through HBM — tiling into SRAM and fusing matmul+softmax+mask+dropout into one kernel [FlashAttention]. This grounds our Tier-3 fusion-friendly refactors AND Tier-2 dtype/placement choices: every byte that never leaves the chip is free.
- **Overlap via pipelining**: FA-3's s-stage circular SMEM buffers overlap stage-j compute with stage-j+1/j+2 loads; ablation shows removing it costs 661→582 TFLOPS (~12%) [FA-3]. This is the device-side twin of our double-buffered input iterator; it also warns that overlap gains are real but modest-sized even in expert hands.
- **Async copy machinery**: Ampere's `cp.async` global→shared copies and Hopper's TMA (bulk, hardware-driven tensor copies) exist to keep loads off the critical path [FA-3]. JAX/XLA users don't emit these directly, but they predict where XLA has headroom we can't reach by hand — relevant to how we set expectations for Stage-1 probes.
- **Bucketing ↔ capacity equations**: FA derives tile shapes from SRAM size [FlashAttention]; Triton exposes tile shapes as first-class tunables and JIT-autotunes them per target [Triton]. Our shape-bucketing (`tiling.bucket`) is the same idea one level up: pick shapes the compiler can tile well instead of recompiling per length.

### Tier 3 — composition changes

- **Recomputation over materialization**: FA's backward recomputes attention probabilities from tiled inputs instead of storing the N×N matrix — trading FLOPs (cheap, abundant) for bytes (expensive, scarce) [FlashAttention]. Directly parallels our remat/checkpoint tradeoff item and the on-the-fly one-hot question.
- **Online aggregation enables fusion of "impossible" ops**: softmax couples an entire row, yet online softmax computes it block-incrementally with running max/sum statistics and rescaling [FlashAttention]. Lesson for us: a reduction that *looks* like a global barrier can often be restructured as a streaming accumulation — the same trick scan-based gradient accumulation exploits.
- **Algorithm/schedule separation as the compiler answer to hand-tuning**: Halide fully separates what from how, letting identical math retarget CPU vector code or CUDA by editing schedule lines [Halide]; Triton declines full separation, keeping schedules implicit and autotuned via parametric tiles [Triton]; Ansor searches schedule spaces automatically, beating hand-written kernels in some regimes [Ansor]. XLA sits closer to the Triton end: implicit scheduling with limited user levers (donate, remat, shard_map) — which is why our taxonomy treats composition changes as the primary lever and documents expectations accordingly.
- **Low precision as a data-movement optimization**: FP16→FP8 halves bytes moved per activation; FA-3's FP8 path reaches 1.2 PFLOPs/s vs 740 TFLOPs/s FP16 [FA-3]. In our terms, dtype choice is legitimately Tier-2/Tier-3 boundary territory with numerics-parity obligations: FP16 safety requires FP32 master weights, FP32 accumulation, and FP32 reductions in sensitive reductions [Micikevicius]; FP8 assigns E4M3 to weights/activations and E5M2 where range matters [FP8 formats].

## (d) Measurement methodology patterns worth stealing for ProbeRecord discipline

These are practices reported by the corpus's most rigorous papers, each mapped to what our ProbeRecord/claims machinery already does or should do:

1. **Lock clocks; report frequency; average many runs.** FlashAttention-3 locks H100 clocks to a fixed 1830 MHz and reports averages over 100 runs [FA-3]; MARLIN sweeps under a locked base clock and shows that prior kernels' relative speedups *distort* under uncapped boost clocks due to timing dependencies [MARLIN]. Implication: our planned Stage-2 GPU re-runs should record `gpu_clock_mhz` / `clock_locked` in the ProbeRecord environment block alongside platform/git_sha, or cross-config rankings are not trustworthy.
2. **Control cache state explicitly.** Ansor flushes caches between timed runs so warm-L3 artifacts cannot fake speedups, enabling accurate measurements with fewer repetitions [Ansor]. Our TwoPhaseTiming already separates compile from run; the analogous move is declaring cold/warm input state per probe driver rather than leaving it ambient.
3. **Report central tendency AND spread.** Ansor plots medians of 5 full tuning runs with standard-deviation error bars [Ansor]; FlashAttention reports end-to-end training time as mean ± std over 10 eight-GPU runs (20.0 ± 1.5 min baseline vs 17.4 ± 1.4 min optimized) [FlashAttention]. Our current records lean on point estimates; before granting TERM_RANKING on GPU re-runs, records should carry an N-run summary (median + dispersion).
4. **Overlay measurements on hardware ceilings.** MARLIN and Triton plot achieved TFLOP/s directly against roofline bandwidth/compute ceilings, proving proximity to physical limits [MARLIN], [Triton]; the Nsight Compute profiler operationalizes exactly this as roofline charts [Nsight Compute]. Our Stage-0 cost probes already estimate cost structure without execution; emitting an arithmetic-intensity estimate and comparing against published platform ceilings would be the same trick at zero runtime cost.
5. **Pair against the strongest plausible baseline, not the default.** MARLIN compares against PyTorch/CUTLASS plus four specialized kernels (AWQ, bitsandbytes, ExLlamaV2) plus an "ideal weight-loading roofline" [MARLIN]; Halide refuses to benchmark against naive C++ and targets Adobe production code tuned over months with OpenMP, heterogeneous CPU+GPU, and IPP assembly [Halide]. Our "baseline first" rule should say explicitly: the baseline is the best currently-shipped configuration, and a winning record against a strawman is invalid.
6. **Honest micro/macro dissection.** vLLM reports its own PagedAttention kernel is 20–26% *slower* than FasterTransformer's contiguous attention kernel in isolation, then shows end-to-end throughput gains of 2–4× because eliminating fragmentation enables much larger batches [PagedAttention/vLLM]. Pattern: when micro and macro disagree, publish both sides as separate measured facts. This is precisely our winner-and-loser-records-both-persist policy, extended with "a Tier-2 change may lose at micro level yet legitimately win END_TO_END — record both, never average them away."
7. **Ablation matrices attribute wins to mechanisms.** FA-3's Table-2-style ablations isolate warp specialization (570→661 TFLOPS) and async pipelining (661→582) as separate removals [FA-3]. This is the literature's version of our one-tier-per-candidate rule: attribution requires removing one thing at a time.
8. **Sweep the domain to find regime shifts.** ADEPT sweeps multiple DNA/protein dataset families of varying query lengths to expose where bottlenecks shift from instruction overhead to compute density [ADEPT]; vLLM sweeps block sizes to locate recomputation-vs-swapping crossover points [PagedAttention/vLLM]. Our shape-bucket boundaries should likewise be placed where measured regimes shift, and probe drivers should declare their shape axis (`xtrax_n_atoms`) so records are comparable within a regime.
9. **Prove numerics separately from speed.** FlashAttention validates end-to-end with full validation-perplexity curves showing optimization did not cause divergence [FlashAttention]. We already gate `parity_max_abs_diff` before measurement; the literature confirms parity evidence belongs in the record, not in an afterthought.

## (e) Contradictions / open debates across sources

1. **Fusion enthusiasm vs separation of concerns.** The fusion camp: FlashAttention exists *only* because matmul+softmax+mask+dropout fuse into one kernel that refuses to materialize O(N²) intermediates [FlashAttention]; XLA's jit aims to trace whole programs into single fused executables and even exposes `MUST_FUSE` hooks [JAX 0.11 ref]; Triton celebrates fusing odd ops (shift-into-conv) to bypass library limitations [Triton]. The separation camp: Halide argues hand-fused pipelines balloon into thousands of lines of interleaved code that destroy portability and composability, hence strict algorithm/schedule separation [Halide]; JAX's own value system is narrow composable transforms over pure functions [JAX 0.11 ref]. Not resolvable in the abstract — Triton's explicit rejection of Halide-style separation shows serious systems disagree [Triton]. Our taxonomy sidesteps the philosophy by making fusion a Tier-3 candidate subject to evidence, not a default virtue.
2. **Autotuning vs hand-tuning.** Autotuner side: Halide bounds its schedule space below at ~10^720 configurations; autotuners routinely pick counter-intuitive winners (sacrificing thread parallelism to cut synchronization, adding redundant compute for SRAM locality) and beat human-optimized pipelines built over weeks by up to 5× [Halide]; Ansor outperforms template-bound autotuners like AutoTVM [Ansor]. Hand-tuning side: FA-3's wins come from exploiting undocumented-feeling Hopper specifics (`setmaxnreg`, mbarriers, TMA) that no generic tuner targets [FA-3]. Open debate; for xtrax it is moot-by-policy (autotuning sweeps are out of scope per our spec) but it warns that XLA's internal autotuning choices make micro-results environment-sensitive — another argument for paired-config unanimity.
3. **Escape hatches vs compiler visibility ("the opaque kernel problem").** Dropping to Pallas/custom kernels buys bare-metal performance at the cost of becoming opaque to XLA: no inter-operator fusion, no global memory planning, no asynchronous scheduling across those boundaries [JAX 0.11 ref]. No source resolves when escape hatches pay off; it is inherently workload-dependent and measurable case-by-case — exactly the kind of question our claim gates exist for.
4. **Compiler automation vs explicit control.** XLA's automatic rematerialization pass reportedly makes poor choices on real models, pushing experts to disable it and coordinate checkpointing manually via name-based policies [JAX 0.11 ref]; yet manual donation/remat carry their own sharp edges (invalidated buffers, keyword-arg exceptions). The corpus repeatedly finds automatic passes good enough until they suddenly are not — supporting our posture of trusting neither defaults nor folklore, only paired measurements.
5. **Occupancy as a target.** Naive doctrine says maximize occupancy; the CUDA C++ Best Practices Guide states higher occupancy does not always improve performance because ILP can hide latency with fewer warps, and forcing occupancy causes register spills to DRAM that negate the benefit [CUDA Best Practices]. Hopper's whitepaper goes further: with TMA doing memory management in hardware, only a fraction of threads need touch HBM at all, decoupling latency hiding from occupancy entirely [H100 whitepaper]. Proxy metrics mislead; wall-clock paired evidence decides — a direct hit on our claim-class design.
