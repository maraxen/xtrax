## (d) Measurement methodology patterns worth stealing for ProbeRecord discipline

These are practices reported by the corpus's most rigorous papers, each mapped to what our ProbeRecord/claims machinery already does or should do:

1. **Lock clocks; report frequency; average many runs.** FlashAttention-3 locks H100 clocks to a fixed 1830 MHz and reports averages over 100 runs [FA-3]; MARLIN sweeps under a locked base clock and shows that prior kernels' relative speedups *distort* under uncapped boost clocks due to timing dependencies [MARLIN]. Implication: our planned Stage-2 GPU re-runs should record `gpu_clock_mhz` / `clock_locked` in the ProbeRecord environment block alongside platform/git_sha, or cross-config rankings are not trustworthy.
2. **Control cache state explicitly.** Ansor flushes caches between timed runs so warm-L3 artifacts cannot fake speedups, enabling accurate measurements with fewer repetitions [Ansor]. Our TwoPhaseTiming already separates compile from run; the analogous move is declaring cold/warm input state per probe driver rather than leaving it ambient.
3. **Report central tendency AND spread.** Ansor plots medians of 5 full tuning runs with standard-deviation error bars [Ansor]; FlashAttention reports end-to-end training time as mean ± std over 10 eight-GPU runs (20.0 ± 1.5 min baseline vs 17.4 ± 1.4 min optimized) [FlashAttention]. Our current records lean on point estimates; before granting TERM_RANKING on GPU re-runs, records should carry an N-run summary (median + dispersion).
4. **Overlay measurements on hardware ceilings.** MARLIN and Triton plot achieved TFLOP/s directly against roofline bandwidth/compute ceilings, proving proximity to physical limits [MARLIN], [Triton]; the Nsight Compute profiler operationalizes exactly this as roofline charts [Nsight Compute]. Our Stage-0 cost probes already estimate cost structure without execution; emitting an arithmetic-intensity estimate and comparing against published platform ceilings would be the same trick at zero runtime cost.
5. **Pair against the strongest plausible baseline, not the default.** MARLIN compares against PyTorch/CUTLASS plus four specialized kernels (AWQ, bitsandbytes, ExLlamaV2) plus an "ideal weight-loading roofline" [MARLIN]; Halide refuses to benchmark against naive C++ and targets Adobe production code tuned over months with OpenMP, heterogeneous CPU+GPU, and IPP assembly [Halide]. Our "baseline first" rule should say explicitly: the baseline is the best currently-shipped configuration, and a winning record against a strawman is invalid.
6. **Honest micro/macro dissection.** vLLM reports its own PagedAttention kernel is 20–26% *slower* than FasterTransformer's contiguous attention kernel in isolation, then shows end-to-end throughput gains of 2–4× because eliminating fragmentation enables much larger batches [PagedAttention/vLLM]. Pattern: when micro and macro disagree, publish both sides as separate measured facts. This is precisely our winner-and-loser-records-both-persist policy, extended with "a Tier-2 change may lose at micro level yet legitimately win END_TO_END — record both, never average them away."
7. **Ablation matrices attribute wins to mechanisms.** FA-3's Table-2-style ablations isolate warp specialization (570→661 TFLOPS) and async pipelining (661→582) as separate removals [FA-3]. This is the literature's version of our one-tier-per-candidate rule: attribution requires removing one thing at a time.
8. **Sweep the domain to find regime shifts.** ADEPT sweeps multiple DNA/protein dataset families of varying query lengths to expose where bottlenecks shift from instruction overhead to compute density [ADEPT]; vLLM sweeps block sizes to locate recomputation-vs-swapping crossover points [PagedAttention/vLLM]. Our shape-bucket boundaries should likewise be placed where measured regimes shift, and probe drivers should declare their shape axis (`xtrax_n_atoms`) so records are comparable within a regime.
9. **Prove numerics separately from speed.** FlashAttention validates end-to-end with full validation-perplexity curves showing optimization did not cause divergence [FlashAttention]. We already gate `parity_max_abs_diff` before measurement; the literature confirms parity evidence belongs in the record, not in an afterthought.

## (e) Contradictions / open debates across sources

1. **Fusion enthusiasm vs separation of concerns.** The fusion camp: FlashAttention exists *only* because matmul+softmax+mask+dropout fuse into one kernel that refuses to materialize O(N²) intermediates [FlashAttention]; XLA's jit aims to trace whole programs into single fused executables and even exposes `MUST_FUSE` hooks [JAX 0.11 ref]; Triton celebrates fusing odd ops (shift-into-conv) to bypass library limitations [Triton]. The separation camp: Halide argues hand-fused pipelines balloon into thousands of lines of interleaved code that destroy portability and composability, hence strict algorithm/schedule separation [Halide]; JAX's own value system is narrow composable transforms over pure functions [JAX 0.11 ref]. Not resolvable in the abstract — Triton's explicit rejection of Halide-style separation shows serious systems disagree [Triton]. Our taxonomy sidesteps the philosophy by making fusion a Tier-3 candidate subject to evidence, not a default virtue.
2. **Autotuning vs hand-tuning.** Autotuner side: Halide bounds its schedule space below at ~10^720 configurations; autotuners routinely pick counter-intuitive winners (sacrificing thread parallelism to cut synchronization, adding redundant compute for SRAM locality) and beat human-optimized pipelines built over weeks by up to 5× [Halide]; Ansor outperforms template-bound autotuners like AutoTVM [Ansor]. Hand-tuning side: FA-3's wins come from exploiting undocumented-feeling Hopper specifics (`setmaxnreg`, mbarriers, TMA) that no generic tuner targets [FA-3]. Open debate; for xtrax it is moot-by-policy (autotuning sweeps are out of scope per our spec) but it warns that XLA's internal autotuning choices make micro-results environment-sensitive — another argument for paired-config unanimity.
3. **Escape hatches vs compiler visibility ("the opaque kernel problem").** Dropping to Pallas/custom kernels buys bare-metal performance at the cost of becoming opaque to XLA: no inter-operator fusion, no global memory planning, no asynchronous scheduling across those boundaries [JAX 0.11 ref]. No source resolves when escape hatches pay off; it is inherently workload-dependent and measurable case-by-case — exactly the kind of question our claim gates exist for.
4. **Compiler automation vs explicit control.** XLA's automatic rematerialization pass reportedly makes poor choices on real models, pushing experts to disable it and coordinate checkpointing manually via name-based policies [JAX 0.11 ref]; yet manual donation/remat carry their own sharp edges (invalidated buffers, keyword-arg exceptions). The corpus repeatedly finds automatic passes good enough until they suddenly are not — supporting our posture of trusting neither defaults nor folklore, only paired measurements.
5. **Occupancy as a target.** Naive doctrine says maximize occupancy; the CUDA C++ Best Practices Guide states higher occupancy does not always improve performance because ILP can hide latency with fewer warps, and forcing occupancy causes register spills to DRAM that negate the benefit [CUDA Best Practices]. Hopper's whitepaper goes further: with TMA doing memory management in hardware, only a fraction of threads need touch HBM at all, decoupling latency hiding from occupancy entirely [H100 whitepaper]. Proxy metrics mislead; wall-clock paired evidence decides — a direct hit on our claim-class design.
