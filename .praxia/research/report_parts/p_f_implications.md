## (f) Concrete implications for xtrax

Each item is tagged **[confirms]** (literature supports a decision already in our scope doc), **[extends]** (new actionable refinement), or **[challenges]** (tension with an assumption we hold).

1. **[confirms]** The memory-bound premise: the corpus's central physical fact is that data movement dominates modern kernels [FlashAttention], [Roofline/Nsight Compute]. Our taxonomy treating T2/T3 as where the leverage lives — and Stage-0 cost probes as a cheap pre-filter — matches how the field reasons.
2. **[confirms]** Fusion-friendly refactors belong in T3 *with* mandatory probes: fusion is the single most validated technique class [FlashAttention], but Halide documents its portability cost [Halide], so evidence-gating rather than reflexive fusing is the defensible stance our spec already takes.
3. **[extends]** Add run-variance fields to ProbeRecord before GPU TERM_RANKING re-runs: N runs, median + dispersion, plus `clock_locked`/`gpu_clock_mhz` environment keys. Directly imitated from FA-3's 100-run locked-clock protocol and MARLIN's demonstration that unlocked clocks distort rankings [FA-3], [MARLIN].
4. **[extends]** Stage-0 probes could emit an arithmetic-intensity estimate per scope term, comparable to published platform ceilings — the zero-execution analogue of MARLIN/Triton roofline overlays and Nsight's roofline charts [MARLIN], [Nsight Compute].
5. **[extends]** Make "baseline = strongest shipped configuration" explicit in measurement-protocol.md; strawman baselines are the field's most common failure mode [MARLIN], [Halide].
6. **[extends]** Our honest negative result on `async_indexed_stream` (0.70× slower on sub-ms CPU steps) mirrors the literature's crossover framing: vLLM publishes recomputation-vs-swapping crossovers across block sizes rather than binary verdicts [PagedAttention/vLLM]. Deliverable: document the expected overlap-beneficial regime (step time ≳ transfer time) in tier2 reference, keeping the driver as the instrument that locates the crossover per platform.
7. **[extends]** Scan-body remat recipe for tier3-composition.md: XLA's automatic remat struggles across structured control flow; manually applying `jax.checkpoint` to `lax.scan` bodies is the standard defense against memory bloat, with name-based policies (`save_only_these_names`, `save_and_offload_only_these_names`) for fine control including host offload of residuals [JAX 0.11 ref]. Since xtrax pipelines are scan-heavy, this is our highest-value documentation addition.
8. **[confirms]** Donation sharp edges justify our narrow donation surface (`sparse_filter_jit` only): donated buffers are invalidated after the call, keyword-passed args are never donated, PyTree donation sweeps every leaf, and unmatched donations are silently dropped with only a warning [JAX 0.11 ref]. A single audited entry point beats scattered donation.
9. **[challenges]** The opaque-kernel cliff: any future custom-op/Pallas escape hatch forfeits XLA's cross-operator fusion and memory planning [JAX 0.11 ref]. If xtrax ever grows a CustomCall path, our DISPATCH_COUNT tripwires must be complemented by a structural check that fusion was actually lost vs merely moved — otherwise a "fast kernel" record can hide a slower program.
10. **[confirms]** One-tier-per-candidate attribution rule is exactly how rigorous papers work: FA-3 ablates warp specialization and pipelining separately [FA-3]; vLLM separates kernel-level from engine-level effects [PagedAttention/vLLM]. Keep refusing mixed-tier candidate changes.
11. **[extends]** Bucket boundaries deserve regime-shift placement: ADEPT's multi-length sweeps show bottleneck identity changes across sequence lengths [ADEPT]; our `tiling.bucket` docs should instruct choosing boundaries at measured regime shifts (via Stage-1 feed/probe pairs), not round numbers.
12. **[confirms]** Proxy-metric skepticism: occupancy-maximization doctrine is explicitly debunked (ILP hides latency; forced occupancy spills registers) [CUDA Best Practices], reinforcing our rule that DISPATCH_COUNT/STRUCTURAL floors are necessary but wall-clock paired evidence decides keeps.
13. **[extends]** Dtype coercion guidance in tier2 can inherit the mixed-precision safety recipe: FP16 needs FP32 master weights + FP32 accumulation + FP32 reductions in sensitive ops [Micikevicius]; FP8 splits into E4M3 (weights/activations) and E5M2 (wider range, gradients) [FP8 formats]. CPU-only today, so ship as documentation with parity-gated probe hooks ready for GPU re-runs.
14. **[challenges]** Autotuner counter-intuitiveness (5× wins over weeks of human tuning; redundant compute chosen deliberately) [Halide] challenges any intuition-first optimization habit — even "obvious" T1/T2 tweaks must produce a supporting record or get reverted. Our fail-closed keep policy is the right institutional answer; apply it without exception to expert-authored candidates too.
15. **[extends]** GDS's design pattern — direct fast path with automatic compatibility fallback when `O_DIRECT` is unavailable [GPUDirect Storage] — legitimizes our tap/sink staging pattern (fast in-graph drain + correct blocking fallback), and suggests documenting both paths' costs side-by-side in tier1 reference.
